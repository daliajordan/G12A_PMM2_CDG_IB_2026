## Background

Phosphomannomutase 2 (PMM2) is a protein whose function is to convert mannose-6-phosphate (M6P) into mannose-1-phosphate (M1P) and requires glucose-1,6-bisphosphate (G16BP) or mannose-1,6-bisphosphate (M16BP) as an activator. The M1P is a precursor of GDP-mannose, which gives mannose to build dolichol-phosphate-mannose (Dol-P-M), which in turn fuels the construction of the glycan that attaches to proteins, N-glycosylation (Oliveira et al. 2025). PMM2 is only active as a dimer, a protein composed of two chains joined together to form a functional unit. PMM2 is a homodimer: its two chains are identical and each one is divided by zones depending on the amino acid positions. Said zones are: the core domain, the cap domain and the linkers 1 and 2 (Briso-Montiano et al. 2022).

The two subunits do not have the same conformation in the dimer and differ in 3D. One of them has its core closer to the cap than the other. This should be expected for an enzyme that opens and closes to trap its substrate, enabled by the linkers, a proposition based on the crystal structure (Briso-Montiano et al. 2022).

PMM2-congenital disorder of glycosylation (PMM2-CDG) is an autosomal recessive disease and the most prevalent N-glycosylation disorder. The disorder is caused by variants in PMM2 that reduce its activity. When PMM2 activity is reduced, the absence of M1P means GDP-mannose is lacking, so proteins receive fewer glycans becoming hypoglycosylated. This translates into a malfunction of several organs, its primary impact being on the nervous system causing disorders such as ataxia or epilepsy (Oliveira et al. 2025).

Furthermore, a way to classify missense variants into six different mechanisms of impact to predict the effects on the function and structure of PMM2 was proposed (Briso-Montiano et al. 2022). These mechanisms of impact were activator binding, catalysis, dimerization, cap folding, core folding, and linker 2.
41 of these pathogenic missense variants taken from ClinVar were analyzed using the wild-type PMM2 structure Protein Data Bank (PDB) entry 7O4G and the PyMOL mutagenesis tool, using the classification proposed by Briso-Montiano et al. as a basis (Oliveira et al. 2025).

The variant studied in this report is the p.Thr237Met (T237M), located on the α9 helix of the core domain of the chain. Based on the superposition of the wild-type (WT) and the T237M, there are no major changes in the overall fold. The dimers are essentially identical, with low Root-mean-square deviation (RMSD) values. The side chain of T237 forms a hydrogen bond with T16, which is situated on the loop that coordinates Mg-1 (the catalytic magnesium ion). By replacing threonine with methionine, the bond is lost, affecting both stability and catalysis (Briso-Montiano et al. 2022).
