Creation of sequence alignments:

	In order to generate the sequence alignments used in this paper, we retrieved the FASTA files of: PMM2 human WT CDS, PMM2 human mutant CDS, PMM2 human WT protein and PMM2 human mutant protein, from internal sources. And the FASTA files of: PMM2 leishmania WT CDS	(XM_003874496.1), PMM2 Vicugna Pacos WT CDS (XM_072942246.1), PMM2 Leishmania Mexicana WT protein (XP_003874545.1) and PMM2 Vicugna Pacos WT (XP_072798347.1) protein from the NCBI database, by looking up the human WT sequences in the BLAST database and limiting the search to the concentrate on each species, while prioritizing somewhat dissimilar sequences.

	For the WT and mutant CDS comparison, we opened the blastn interface, added the human WT CDS to the query block, ticked the box that says "Align two or more sequences" and adde the mutant CDS to Subject Sequence block. We then BLASTed the query. The same process was used to create the sequence alignment for the proteins, except with the blastp interface instead of the blastn one. Creating two separate alignments, one for the CDS sequences and another for the protein sequences.

	For the ortholog comparison, we first cut the Vicugna CDS FASTA file down to its coding nucleotides as it was otherwise 5795 nucleotides long, 5000 nucleotides longer than the other two sequences, with most codons being after a stop one. The alignment itself we did with the Clustal Omega sequence alignment tool (CLUSTAL O(1.2.4) multiple sequence alignment). By adding first the Human WT sequence, then the Leishmania one, then the Vicugna one and starting the job. Again, creating two separate alignments, one for the CDS sequences and another for the protein sequences.

 Analysis of the sequence alignments:

	We then analyzed these alignments in order to get results.

	For the WT vs mutants comparisons, we looked at the sequence alignments using the "Pairwise with dots for identities" alignment view, in order to more easily see and locate differences. For the CDS comparison specifically, we used the ___ tool to separate the CDS sequences by codons in order to be able to identify which codon the mutation was part of.

	For the ortholog CDS comparison, the Clustal Omega aligning tool marks under each aligned nucleotide whether the nucleotides coincide (with an asterisk) or don't (with a space), we looked at the clusters of asterisks in order to determine highly conserved regions, and at the aligned sequences in order to determine of which type the non-conserved regions were.

	For the ortholog protein comparison, the Clustal Omega aligning tool marks under each aligned protein whether they are the same (with an asterisk), of the same physicochemical group (with a colon) or otherwise (with a space or dot). We looked at the concentration of asterisks and colons in order to determine and analyze conservation.

Analysis of the structural alignments and literature:

	The analysis above was complemented with a report on the structural alignment of the PMM2 Human protein WT sequence and the Leishmania mexicana protein sequence, provided by internal sources. This report includes statistical data on sequence identity, percentage of proteins with coinciding physicochemical class, Global RMSD, primary and secondary structure alignment, local backbone displacement, and 3D imaging of the two proteins superimposed. It also includes information on the disease-associated variant of the protein, including the protein mutation type and place, local backbone displacement of the variant, global WT-Mutant RMSD and imaging of the WT and mutant proteins superimposed, specifically around the mutated region.

	We also continuously used information found on the PDB page associated to our mutated protein (7O58).
