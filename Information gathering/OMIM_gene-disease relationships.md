PMM2 is a gene that codifies the protein phosphomannomutase 2 (PMM2), an enzyme that helps to conduct the reaction of glycosylation (adding chains of sugar to proteins). It’s located in the chromosome 16, specifically in 16p13.2. 

PMM2 catalyses mannose-6-phosphate to mannose-1-phosphate, which is used for the GDP-mannose, a molecule important for glycosylation. It’s important for protein stability and molecular functions. Disruption of this process can affect the function of a normal protein, not producing molecules for the reactions leading to abnormal cellular activity. The deficiency of the enzyme phosphomannomutase 2 decreases the production of mannose-1-phosphate and causes malfunctions, the PMM2-CDG. Known as congenital disorder of glycosylation, it’s an autosomal recessive disorder, meaning that it needs 2 copies of the gene. 

Variants can affect the PMM2 structure and functionality. Oliveira et al. (2025) investigated the missense variants and arrived at the conclusion that to investigate the functional effects, it should be considered also other variants of the patient, symptoms , the 2 copies of the mutants and molecular changes. They may have a relation to the impact. 

They documented 41 missense replacements. For this gene it has been documented to be different types, but our case is a missense, a change of nucleotide by another. In which in the position 710 the nucleotide Cytosine has been changed for a Timine affecting the codon causing a change in the position 237 of the protein, of an Threonine by a Methionine, responsible for the variant T237M. It has been reported in individuals who have PMM2-CDG. 

Since these amino acids have different chemical properties, a change could provoke unfavorable interactions between amino acids, can influence the stability and the function of the protein. It can reduce catalytic activity of the enzyme, affect the stability of the protein, the folding, union to the substrate and interaction with other molecules.

The Threonine is polar and has a Hydroxyl group which allows it to form Hydrogen bonds. On the other hand, Methionine isn’t polar and doesn’t have a hydroxyl group. So, it can’t form this bond.  

By the studies of Briso-Montiano et al. (2022) of the structure of the wild-type and variant PMM2, its replacement could disrupt a hydrogen bond involving Thr237 and Thr16 affecting the catalytic activity ,stability and dimerization of the protein. However, there aren’t great changes in the overall structure of the protein. 

“The disease is characterized by defective glycosylation of glycoconjugates. It usually presents as a severe disorder in the neonatal period. There is a severe encephalopathy with axial hypotonia, abnormal eye movement, and pronounced psychomotor retardation, as well as peripheral neuropathy, cerebellar hypoplasia, and retinitis pigmentosa. Patients show a peculiar distribution of subcutaneous fat, nipple retraction, and hypogonadism.” (Oliveira et al,2025)
